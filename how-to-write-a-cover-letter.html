How to Write a Cover Letter That Actually Gets Read

ResumeMaker Blog · 14 November 2026 · 6 min read

Many job seekers in India treat the cover letter as an afterthought — a few copied lines attached to a resume. But when a job posting asks for one, the cover letter is your first chance to speak directly to the hiring manager. A good one explains, in your own words, why you want this job and why you are a good fit. A bad one is skipped in seconds. This guide shows you how to write a cover letter that actually gets read.

What a Cover Letter Is For

Your resume lists facts: education, skills, experience. Your cover letter explains them. It answers three questions the resume cannot: why this company, why this role, and why you. Keep that purpose in mind for every sentence you write. If a sentence does not help answer one of those questions, it probably does not belong in the letter.

The Structure That Works

A strong cover letter follows a simple structure. Start with your contact details and the date, then address the letter to a real person whenever you can find a name — "Dear Ms. Sharma" is far better than "Dear Sir/Madam". Then write three or four short paragraphs: an opening that says which role you are applying for and why it caught your eye; one or two middle paragraphs that connect your skills and experience to the job; and a short closing that asks for an interview and thanks the reader. Sign off politely with your name and phone number. The whole letter should fit on one page.

Research the Company First

Before you write, spend fifteen minutes learning about the company: what it does, who its customers are, and what it has been working on recently. Then mention one specific thing in your letter — a product launch, a new branch, a value the company talks about — and connect it to your own skills. This single detail proves you wrote the letter for this job, not for fifty jobs at once. Recruiters notice it immediately.

Tell One Story, Not Your Whole Resume

The most common mistake is repeating the resume in paragraph form. Your cover letter should not list everything you have done. Instead, pick one or two experiences that matter most for this role and tell them briefly as a story: the situation, what you did, and what happened as a result. A fresher might describe a final-year project or an internship; an experienced candidate might describe a problem they solved at work. One good story is more convincing than ten bullet points repeated in prose.

Keep Your Tone Professional and Human

Write the way a confident, polite person speaks. Avoid stiff phrases like "I am writing to humbly submit my application" and avoid slang. Be direct: "I am applying for the Accounts Assistant role because my Tally and GST training matches exactly what your posting asks for." Use the company's language where it fits, and write in the first person. A cover letter is a letter — it should sound like a person, not a template.

Mistakes That Get Letters Ignored

Do not copy the same letter for every job — recruiters can tell. Do not write more than one page. Do not start with your life story or your salary expectations. Do not use a generic greeting if the hiring manager's name is easy to find on the company website or job posting. And never send a cover letter with a spelling mistake: proofread it the same way you proofread your resume, and read it aloud once before sending.

A Simple Outline to Follow

If you are stuck on a blank page, follow this outline. Paragraph one: the role, where you found it, and one line on why it interests you. Paragraph two: your most relevant experience or skill, with one concrete example. Paragraph three: what you know about the company and why you want to work there. Paragraph four: a short closing — you would welcome an interview, thank you for your time. Four paragraphs, one page, your own words. That is all a cover letter needs to be.
