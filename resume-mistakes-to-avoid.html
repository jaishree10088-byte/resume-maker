10 Resume Mistakes to Avoid (and How to Fix Them)

ResumeMaker Blog · 14 November 2026 · 7 min read

A resume is a short document, but small mistakes in it can cost you interviews. Recruiters see hundreds of resumes for a single opening, and they use mistakes as a quick way to narrow the pile. The good news is that most resume mistakes are easy to fix once you know what they are. Here are the ten most common ones — and what to do instead.

1. Spelling and Grammar Mistakes

A single typo can end your chances, especially for roles that need attention to detail. Fix it by reading your resume aloud, using a spellchecker, and asking someone else to read it. Pay special attention to company names, job titles and dates — spellcheckers do not catch those.

2. One Resume for Every Job

Sending the same resume to a bank, a call centre and a software company tells each recruiter that you did not care enough to adjust it. Fix it by keeping one master resume, then tailoring the summary and skills for each application to match the job description.

3. Too Long, Too Much

A three-page resume from a fresher suggests you cannot prioritise. Fix it by cutting everything that does not prove you can do this job: old school details, long hobby lists, and paragraphs that repeat what a bullet point already says. One page is the target for freshers; experienced candidates can take two.

4. Fancy Formatting That Breaks

Coloured tables, text boxes, icons and unusual fonts may look creative, but they often break when the file is opened on another computer or read by screening software. Fix it with a clean, simple layout: standard headings, one font, and plenty of white space.

5. Exaggerating or Inventing Facts

Claiming a skill you do not have or a job you never held is the fastest way to lose an offer — background checks and interviews catch it. Fix it by being honest and specific about what you actually did. Real, modest achievements beat impressive-sounding inventions every time.

6. Duties Instead of Achievements

"Responsible for handling customers" describes a duty; it does not show what you achieved. Fix it by rewriting each point as an action with a result: "Handled 30 customer calls a day and resolved billing queries, with no complaints escalated in two months."

7. Weak, Passive Language

Words like "helped with", "was involved in" and "responsible for" make your work sound small. Fix it with strong action verbs: built, prepared, organised, managed, trained, improved, delivered. Start each bullet point with a verb.

8. Unnecessary Personal Details

Many Indian resumes still list religion, marital status, father's name and a full home address. None of these help you get hired, and some can invite unfair judgement. Fix it by keeping only what an employer needs: your name, phone number, email, city and a LinkedIn profile if you have one.

9. An Unprofessional Email Address

An email address from your school days can make a bad first impression before anyone reads a word. Fix it by creating a simple address with your name in it, and check that inbox daily while you are job hunting.

10. No Keywords From the Job Description

If the job asks for "GST filing" and your resume only says "accounts work", both the software and the recruiter may miss the match. Fix it by using the exact skill and tool names from the posting — honestly, where they apply to you.

None of these mistakes is hard to fix, and fixing them costs nothing but a careful hour of editing. Before you send your next application, read your resume once with this list beside you. Every mistake you remove is one more reason for a recruiter to keep your resume in the "yes" pile.
