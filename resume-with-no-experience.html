How to Write a Resume With No Experience: A Guide for Students and Freshers

ResumeMaker Blog · 14 November 2026 · 6 min read

"I have no experience — so what do I put on my resume?" This is the question almost every student and fresher asks, and the answer is simpler than it feels: experience does not only mean a job. Your education, projects, internships, volunteering and skills are all real experience, and a good resume shows them properly. This guide explains how.

Lead With Education and Projects

When you have no job history, your education and your projects go first. List your degree or diploma with the institution, year and marks or CGPA if they are good. Then give your best one or two academic projects the space a job would normally get: a short title, one line on what the project was, what you built or did, and the tools you used. A final-year project, a college website, a billing app for a family shop, a data analysis exercise — all of these count, as long as you describe them concretely.

Count Internships, Volunteering and Part-Time Work

Many freshers forget that short internships, volunteer work and part-time jobs are experience. A two-month internship at a CA firm, a summer spent teaching children at an NGO, a part-time job at a mobile shop, or helping run a college festival all show that you can work with people, handle responsibility and finish tasks. Describe them like jobs: your role, what you did, and what came of it. Even a short stint, described well, tells a recruiter more than a blank experience section.

Build a Skills Section That Means Something

Your skills section should list things you can actually do, not words you have heard. Instead of writing "computer skills", name the tools: MS Excel, Tally, Canva, HTML, Python, or whatever you genuinely know. If you learned a skill through a course, say so — and be ready to demonstrate it. A short course certificate in digital marketing, data entry, or spoken English is worth listing, because it shows initiative. But only list skills you can defend in an interview; claiming a language or tool you barely know will be found out quickly.

Add Activities and Leadership

College life gives you more to show than you think. Organising a cultural event, leading a team in a competition, running a college club, managing a social media page for a society, or playing in a sports team all demonstrate teamwork, planning and leadership. Pick the one or two activities where you actually did something — organised, led, built or managed — and describe them in one line each. This section is especially useful for freshers because it shows character, not just qualifications.

Write a Strong Summary

With no experience to summarise, your opening lines should say what you studied, what you can do, and what kind of role you are looking for. For example: "BCA graduate with strong skills in Excel and data entry, looking for a back-office role. Completed a three-month internship at a local accounting firm and built a small inventory app as a final-year project." Two or three honest lines like these give the recruiter a reason to keep reading.

Keep the Format Clean and Honest

Use the same clean format as any good resume: one page, clear headings, one font, and no fancy graphics. Never invent a job or stretch a one-week workshop into "six months of experience" — background checks and interviews catch this, and it ends your chances. Your resume does not need to pretend you are experienced. It needs to show that you are ready, capable and honest — and for a first job, that is exactly what employers are looking for.

A Sample Outline for Your First Resume

Here is a structure that works for most freshers: your name and contact details at the top; a two-line summary; a skills section with the tools you know; a projects section with one or two projects described properly; education with your degree, institution and year; then internships, volunteering or activities; and finally any certifications. One page, in that order, and you have a resume that a recruiter can read in seconds and remember.
